# How Storytelling Works with Spatial Information

<!-- Drafted topics only. Content to come. -->

A scan records what a place looks like. It does not, on its own, say what happened there, who it mattered to, or what changed. This guide is about the join: how text, photographs, video and recorded voice attach to a captured space, and what each of them does that the scan cannot.

---

## Why a scan is not yet a story

*A reader given only a space will look around it and leave. What the scan actually provides, and what it withholds.*

## What the scan contributes that other media cannot

*Extent, adjacency and relative position — the sense of how far, how enclosed, what is next to what, and where the reader is standing when they are told something.*

## What each medium contributes

### Text

*Naming, dates, chronology, attribution, and anything that has to be asserted rather than shown. Also the shortest to read and the easiest to over-supply.*

### Photographs

*Time depth: what stood here before, what has been removed, what the scan cannot resolve. The single most useful medium for showing change.*

### Video

*Process and duration — craft, ritual, machinery, weather, anything that only exists as action and disappears in a still.*

### Voice

*Testimony, and presence without a portrait. Accent, hesitation and the pace of someone remembering carry information no transcript keeps.*

## Anchoring media to space

*The four things media can attach to — a point, a surface, an object, or a viewpoint — and how the choice changes what the reader understands the media to be about.*

## Sequence and freedom

*The tension between an authored route and open exploration: what each does to comprehension, and how to combine them without the reader getting lost or railroaded.*

## Density and pacing

*How much to attach and where to attach nothing. Empty space as pacing rather than as an unfinished section.*

## Setting or subject

*When the place is the thing being described, and when it is the room a different story is told in. These call for different treatments.*

## Working with gaps

*What was not scanned, what could not be photographed, what no one is left to narrate. Marking absence honestly rather than papering over it.*

## Fidelity, provenance and honesty

*Reconstruction versus reconstruction-that-looks-plausible. Attribution of sources, dating of media, and how to signal uncertainty inside an environment that reads as evidence.*

## Consent and rights, per medium

*Voice and video carry obligations that text and site photography usually do not. What has to be settled before publication, and what [04 — Story Collection and Interviews](04-story-collection-and-interviews.md) should have secured on the day.*

## Accessibility

*Transcripts, captions, and what a reader gets who cannot or will not navigate a 3D space. The non-spatial fallback is not optional.*

## A worked example

*One place, carried end to end: what was scanned, what was attached, where, and why.*
